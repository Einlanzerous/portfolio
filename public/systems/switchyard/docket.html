Docket · surface 6

The self-assessment docket — findings as tickets, deltas as the reading

Five frames: the monthly one-pager (the thing you read eleven months a year), the quarterly edition, a finding in an ordinary ticket list, a finding as a ticket detail page, and per-project scope + finding history. It reuses the delivery layer's contract exactly — 2.5px state edge on every row, coral means a person is needed, amber means advisory, dashed means something is missing that you should create. One new class prefix (.dk*), no new hues.

Your sketch didn't come through — this is built from the structure you described. If the sketch differs on chrome, the content order and the seam/counted/judged rules here should still hold.

01
Nav: no new top-level item. It lives under LLM Insights. That nav item already means "what the machines and I did"; a docket is its judged form. Sub-tabs become Sessions · Docket · Instruments. A seventh top-level item for something you open four times a year is how you get a nav that means nothing.
02
The habit answer is not chrome, it's a ticket. The edition is itself a ticket assigned to you with a due date, so it arrives through the machinery you already open daily — it shows up in your list next to SY-1188 and nags like everything else. The persistent affordance is then allowed to be tiny: one coral pill in the topbar, 4 findings open · Q3 due 12d. Count, not thesis. A thesis sentence in the chrome gets read twice and becomes furniture; a count that changes stays legible. It sits in the topbar rather than the sidebar because the sidebar is a place-list and this is a state.
03
On the schema: findings as tickets, yes — but two records you didn't ask for. Ticket type finding is right, and it buys you the best property in the design: moving is not authorable. It's computed from the linked instrument's last two readings, so you cannot claim progress in prose. Where the pure-ticket model gets ugly is the other two rules. (a) An edition has fields no ticket has — period, thesis, confounder, grade marks, ledger scope — and a finding is ruled on per edition, so the ruling is a join row, not a ticket status. Force it into a ticket and you're authoring docket state twice, which is the exact thing you're avoiding. (b) "An instrument with no finding is decoration" requires an instrument you can see orphaned. If instruments are fields on findings, an orphan is unrepresentable and the rule can never fire. So: tickets(type=finding) + editions + instruments + rulings(edition, finding, state, note). Three small tables, and both of your rules become renderable instead of remembered.
04
Per-project: your assumption is right, with one correction. Filter on the same list, plus a strip in the project view — no second report. The correction: a filtered docket must not hide the global findings, because then the project view lies by omission and you read "2 open" when 6 are. Filtered views keep a dim band at the bottom — not project-scoped: prompt length, secret pastes, confusion markers — present, unopinionated, unclickable-looking. Frame 5.
05
The detail page keeps the instrument, but as a strip, not a rail. Deferring it to the edition breaks the ticket: the instrument is the only thing that makes a finding falsifiable, and a page that shows a claim with no instrument reads as an opinion. What the detail page does drop is the delta reading — the strip shows the current value and the definition, and the sparkline-with-seam lives in the edition where neighbouring findings give it scale. A capture gap renders as a dashed strip that offers to create the instrument. Frame 4.
06
One thing I'd push back on harder than you asked. "Length is a feature" needs an enforcement surface or it dies by edition four. The monthly one-pager is capped by construction — it has to fit one screen, so a fourteenth finding physically cannot be added without closing one. Let that be the real limit: the docket has a fixed number of slots. A new finding costs you an old one, which is also the only mechanism that makes you rule on the boring ones. The quarterly edition is allowed to be long; the docket is not allowed to grow.
Monthly one-pager  — states only. No prose, no grade, no scrolling. Eleven months a year this is the whole product.

Docket state is derived: open = backlog or blocked · moving = in progress and the instrument moved the right way in the last two readings · closed = closed with a resolution · recanted = closed with resolution recanted. Nothing about docket state is typed by hand, which is the whole argument for putting findings in the ticket table.
